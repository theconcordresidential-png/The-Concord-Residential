Learn More About How We Handle Handyman Work

We Walk The Job First

Before we touch a tool, we walk the job with you. We look at what needs fixing, talk about what you want it to look like when we are done, and tell you upfront if anything needs a licensed trade like electrical or plumbing that falls outside handyman work.

Drywall Repairs

For holes and cracks, we cut back the damaged drywall to a clean edge and add a backing board behind the hole so the patch has something to hold onto. Then we tape the seams and apply two to three thin coats of joint compound, sanding lightly between each coat. The last step is priming the patch so it takes paint the same way as the rest of the wall, not a different texture underneath.

Paint Touch Ups

We match your existing paint color and sheen as close as we can get. Flat, eggshell, satin, and gloss all reflect light differently, and using the wrong sheen is one of the most common reasons a touch up ends up looking worse than the damage. We clean the surface first too, since dust and grease keep new paint from sticking evenly.

Fixture And Faucet Installs

Before we install anything, we shut the water off at the source, not just at the fixture. Threaded connections get plumber's tape to stop leaks, and we check the water pressure once everything is hooked back up.

Doors, Trim, And Small Carpentry

For sticking doors, we check the hinges first since loose screws are the most common cause. If the door has swollen from moisture, we plane down the high spot instead of forcing it shut. For trim work, we measure twice and cut once, filling nail holes before anything gets painted.

Liberty's Historic Homes Need A Different Toolkit

Working around the historic districts near Liberty's Courthouse Square means dealing with plaster walls instead of drywall, original trim profiles that aren't sold at any home improvement store anymore, and door hardware that's older than most of the tools we carry. Repairs in these homes take more patience and often more improvisation than the same job in a newer subdivision.

Gladstone's Mid Century Ranches

A lot of Gladstone's housing stock dates back to the mid twentieth century, with mid century ranch homes making up a big share of the streets near Oak Grove Park and Maple Woods. These homes tend to have their own quirks, from older electrical fixtures to trim styles that fell out of production decades ago, which shapes how we source materials and match finishes on repair work here.

North Kansas City's Mixed Residential And Commercial Buildings

Along and around Armour Road, we see a mix of residential upkeep and light commercial repair work, from apartment units above storefronts to the small businesses themselves. That mix means switching between different types of jobs even within the same day, something the Northtown area sees more of than a purely residential suburb.

Newer Neighborhoods In Overland Park

Overland Park's newer subdivisions bring a different kind of handyman call, less about repairing decades old damage and more about fixture upgrades, furniture assembly, and finishing touches on homes that are still relatively young. The work moves faster, but there's often more of it at once, especially right after a move in.

Lee's Summit's Growing Neighborhoods

Lee's Summit has grown considerably around its historic downtown and train depot, and that growth means we're just as likely to be patching drywall in a newer subdivision near Longview as we are doing detail carpentry work on an older home closer to the original town center.

St. Joseph's Century Old Trim And Woodwork

St. Joseph's historic districts around Museum Hill and Robidoux Hill are full of original wood trim, built in cabinetry, and door hardware that's a hundred years old or more. Matching that kind of detail work takes a different level of care than a standard repair, and it's some of the most detailed carpentry work we take on anywhere in our service area.

Shawnee's Range Of Home Ages

Because Shawnee spans everything from farmhouses that predate its incorporation to brand new subdivisions, our handyman calls here vary more than almost anywhere else we work, sometimes adjusting technique and materials from one appointment to the next based purely on how old the specific home is.

Plattsburg And The Smaller Clinton County Towns

Being based near Plattsburg means a steady stream of handyman work in genuinely old homes, the same Victorian and Steamboat Gothic houses featured on the local historic homes tour, along with similar properties in Cameron and Kearney. These calls often involve trim, door, and window repairs on materials that simply aren't manufactured anymore, which means more hands on fabrication than a straightforward parts swap.

A Drywall Patch In A Liberty Bungalow

A typical drywall call near Liberty's historic district often starts with plaster, not standard drywall, especially in the bungalows and cottages built close to the Square. We test a hidden section first to see whether we're patching plaster or a later drywall retrofit, since the two need completely different repair techniques even though they end up looking the same once painted.

Fixture Swaps In A Gladstone Ranch Home

A faucet or fixture install in one of Gladstone's mid century ranch homes usually means dealing with original supply lines that have never been touched since the house was built. We budget extra time on these calls specifically to check those connections for corrosion before reusing them, since a fitting that's fifty years old doesn't always seal the way a modern one would right out of the box.

A Multi Stop Day In Overland Park

Handyman days in Overland Park often look different from a single job elsewhere, since newer subdivisions mean we're regularly stacking two or three smaller calls back to back within the same neighborhood, a furniture assembly here, a trim repair there. The newer construction means less improvisation and more straightforward, quick turnaround work compared to an older home across the state line.

Trim Work In A St. Joseph Historic Home

Matching trim in a home near St. Joseph's Museum Hill usually means we can't just grab a standard profile off the shelf. We measure the existing molding and either source a matching profile or, on the more detailed jobs, have a section custom milled, since a lot of the trim in this part of the city predates anything sold today.

Repairs In Independence's Truman Heritage District

Homes near the Truman Heritage District, just west of Independence Square, share the same late Victorian character found throughout the Harry S. Truman Historic District. Handyman calls here often involve original hardware, plaster walls, and trim profiles that need the same careful matching we do in Liberty rather than a standard modern replacement part.

Excelsior Springs' Resort Era Buildings

Excelsior Springs still has homes and small buildings dating back to its heyday as a mineral water resort town in the early 1900s. Repair work here sometimes means working around architectural details tied to that resort era, from ornate trim to older window styles that aren't manufactured anymore.

Bungalow Repairs In Brookside And Waldo

The classic bungalows and Tudor style homes throughout Brookside and Waldo were mostly built in the 1920s and 1930s, which puts them in a similar age range to a lot of what we work on in Gladstone. Plaster walls, original woodwork, and older door hardware are the norm here rather than the exception.

Seasonal Timing For Repairs Across The Metro

Spring and early summer bring the heaviest call volume for exterior repairs, doors, trim, and fixture work, as homeowners get outside and notice what winter left behind. We plan our scheduling capacity around that seasonal push rather than staffing evenly across the whole year.

Furniture Assembly Around New Construction In Overland Park

New home communities in Overland Park generate a steady stream of furniture assembly and mounting calls, since move in day almost always means a truck full of flat pack furniture and a new TV that needs to go up safely. These calls tend to cluster right after a new phase of a development opens.

Working Around Lee's Summit's Historic Train Depot

The homes closest to Lee's Summit's historic downtown train depot, built in the early 1900s, carry the same kind of age related repair needs as properties near Liberty Square, older trim, original hardware, and plaster construction that calls for a slower, more careful approach.

Rural Repairs Near Kearney's James Farm

Properties near the James family farm and the surrounding rural parts of Kearney often include outbuildings and older farmhouse style construction alongside the main residence, which means our handyman calls out this way sometimes cover more ground, literally, than a typical suburban repair visit.

Trim Matching Near St. Joseph's Wyeth-Tootle Mansion

Homes near landmarks like the Wyeth-Tootle Mansion in St. Joseph often have the kind of detailed millwork that simply isn't available at a standard hardware store. On these jobs we spend real time upfront figuring out whether a profile can be matched with stock trim or needs to be custom milled.

Faucet And Fixture Work In Shawnee's Older Farmhouses

Shawnee's farming history means some of its oldest homes still have plumbing that's been updated piecemeal over decades rather than all at once. Fixture installs in these homes often turn up a surprise fitting or an outdated valve that needs attention before the new fixture can go in properly.

A Steady Stream Of Small Jobs Near Plattsburg

Being based near Plattsburg means a lot of our weekly schedule is built around small, varied repair calls throughout Clinton County, a door here, a faucet there, sometimes several stops in the same small town on the same day given how close together these communities sit.
